# HAZWOPER PPE Levels Quiz (Levels A, B, C and D)

Free practice quiz · 10 questions

Answer every question, then press **Check my answers** to see your score and an explanation for each one. Study first with the guide: [PPE Levels A, B, C and D Explained (EPA and OSHA HAZWOPER)](https://www.hazwoperosha.com/guides/ppe-levels-a-b-c-d/).

**1. What makes Level A different from Level B?**

- Level A uses an air-purifying respirator
- Level A uses a totally encapsulating, vapor-tight suit
- Level A has no gloves
- Level B uses a supplied-air hood only

**Answer: Level A uses a totally encapsulating, vapor-tight suit.** Both use SCBA or supplied air. Level A adds a vapor-tight, fully encapsulating suit.

**2. Which level is the minimum recommended for initial entry to a site with unknown hazards?**

- Level A
- Level B
- Level C
- Level D

**Answer: Level B.** Level B is the minimum recommended for initial site entry until hazards are identified.

**3. Level C may be used only when:**

- Oxygen is below 19.5%
- Concentrations are above IDLH
- Contaminants and concentrations are known and APR criteria are met
- The chemical is unknown

**Answer: Contaminants and concentrations are known and APR criteria are met.** Level C requires known contaminants, oxygen of at least 19.5%, concentrations below IDLH, and APR criteria met.

**4. Which respirator is part of Level B?**

- N95 filtering facepiece
- Half-mask APR
- Positive-pressure SCBA
- No respirator

**Answer: Positive-pressure SCBA.** Level B uses positive-pressure SCBA or supplied air with escape SCBA.

**5. Level D protection is appropriate when:**

- There is no known atmospheric hazard
- Vapors are present at the PEL
- Oxygen is deficient
- Skin-absorbable chemicals are present

**Answer: There is no known atmospheric hazard.** Level D is a work uniform for sites with no atmospheric hazard and only nuisance contamination.

**6. Which level is used for an oxygen-deficient atmosphere where skin hazard is low?**

- Level A
- Level B
- Level C
- Level D

**Answer: Level B.** Oxygen deficiency needs an atmosphere-supplying respirator; with low skin hazard, Level B fits.

**7. Air-purifying respirators can be used in atmospheres that are IDLH.**

- True
- False

**Answer: False.** False. Only SCBA or supplied air with escape SCBA is used at or above IDLH.

**8. Why is heat stress a major concern in Level A and B?**

- The suits are heavy metal
- Chemical-protective clothing traps body heat
- Respirators cause dehydration
- It isn't a concern

**Answer: Chemical-protective clothing traps body heat.** Impermeable clothing prevents cooling by evaporation, so work-rest cycles and fluids are needed.

**9. What is a PAPR?**

- Positive air pressure regulator
- Powered air-purifying respirator
- Personal air pollution recorder
- Protective apron and respirator

**Answer: Powered air-purifying respirator.** A powered air-purifying respirator blows filtered air into the facepiece or hood. It is an air-purifying respirator, used in Level C.

**10. What can trigger a change in PPE level during work?**

- Air monitoring results
- The time of day
- The day of the week
- Nothing; the level is fixed

**Answer: Air monitoring results.** Monitoring results, new site data and changes in tasks can raise or lower the level, according to the site plan action levels.
