# HAZWOPER 40 Hour Practice Test

Free practice quiz · 15 questions

Answer every question, then press **Check my answers** to see your score and an explanation for each one. Study first with the guide: [What Is HAZWOPER? The Complete Guide to OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120](https://www.hazwoperosha.com/guides/what-is-hazwoper/).

**1. What does HAZWOPER stand for?**

- Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response
- Hazardous Work Operations and Personnel Response
- Hazardous Waste and Pollution Emergency Regulations
- Hazard Operations and Worker Protection Emergency Rules

**Answer: Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response.** HAZWOPER is Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response, OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120.

**2. How much supervised field experience does a 40-hour general site worker need?**

- None
- 1 day
- 3 days
- 5 days

**Answer: 3 days.** 1910.120(e)(3)(i) requires 40 hours of off-site instruction plus at least 3 days of actual field experience under a trained, experienced supervisor.

**3. How often is HAZWOPER refresher training required?**

- Every 3 years
- Every 2 years
- Every year
- Only when changing jobs

**Answer: Every year.** 1910.120(e)(8) requires 8 hours of refresher training annually.

**4. Which PPE level gives the highest level of skin, eye and respiratory protection?**

- Level A
- Level B
- Level C
- Level D

**Answer: Level A.** Level A combines SCBA or supplied air with a totally encapsulating, vapor-tight suit.

**5. Below what oxygen concentration is an atmosphere considered oxygen deficient?**

- 23.5%
- 20.9%
- 19.5%
- 16%

**Answer: 19.5%.** OSHA defines oxygen deficient as less than 19.5% oxygen by volume. Normal air is about 20.9%.

**6. In which work zone does decontamination take place?**

- Exclusion zone
- Contamination reduction zone
- Support zone
- Staging zone

**Answer: Contamination reduction zone.** The decon line sits in the contamination reduction zone (warm zone), between the exclusion and support zones.

**7. Where should the support zone be located?**

- Downwind of the exclusion zone
- Inside the exclusion zone
- Upwind of the exclusion zone
- At the lowest point on the site

**Answer: Upwind of the exclusion zone.** The support zone is clean and placed upwind so vapors and dust drift away from it.

**8. Which PPE level uses an air-purifying respirator?**

- Level A
- Level B
- Level C
- Level D

**Answer: Level C.** Level C uses an air-purifying respirator, allowed only when contaminants are known and APR criteria are met.

**9. What is the buddy system?**

- Two workers share one respirator
- Workers are organized so each is observed by at least one other worker
- A supervisor checks every worker once per shift
- Workers rotate between zones every hour

**Answer: Workers are organized so each is observed by at least one other worker.** Under 1910.120(a)(3), the buddy system organizes employees so each one is observed by at least one other in the group.

**10. How many additional hours of training do on-site supervisors need beyond worker training?**

- 4 hours
- 8 hours
- 16 hours
- 24 hours

**Answer: 8 hours.** 1910.120(e)(4) requires at least 8 additional hours of specialized training for managers and supervisors.

**11. Which of these must be in the site-specific safety and health plan?**

- Employee salary information
- Decontamination procedures
- The company's marketing plan
- Vehicle maintenance logs

**Answer: Decontamination procedures.** Decontamination procedures are one of the required HASP elements in 1910.120(b)(4)(ii).

**12. A combustible gas reading above what percentage of the LEL commonly means workers should withdraw?**

- 1%
- 5%
- 25%
- 100%

**Answer: 25%.** Common EPA guidance treats readings above 25% LEL as an explosion hazard requiring withdrawal. Your site plan sets the exact action levels.

**13. Medical surveillance is required for employees who wear a respirator for how many days or more per year?**

- 5
- 10
- 30
- 90

**Answer: 30.** 1910.120(f)(2) covers employees who wear respirators 30 days or more a year, among other groups.

**14. What is the most common route of exposure at hazardous waste sites?**

- Ingestion
- Injection
- Inhalation
- Hearing

**Answer: Inhalation.** Inhalation of gases, vapors, mists and dusts is the most common route of exposure.

**15. What does IDLH stand for?**

- Initial Daily Limit of Hazard
- Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health
- Industrial Dose Level for Humans
- Inhalation Dose Limit, Hourly

**Answer: Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health.** IDLH is Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health. Only atmosphere-supplying respirators are used at or above IDLH.
